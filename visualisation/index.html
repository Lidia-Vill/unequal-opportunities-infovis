IRELAND'S LABOUR MARKET · 2019–2025

Unequal Opportunities

Has Ireland's employment growth benefited everyone equally?

Ireland's labour market has improved since 2019, but national figures do not tell the whole story. Employment opportunities can vary depending on someone's stage of life, educational qualifications and where they live, while differences between men and women remain an important measure of labour market inequality. Looking beyond the national figures, let's look into how these differences have evolved over time.

Overall, Ireland's labour market appears to be in a stronger position than it was in 2019, despite the disruption caused by the pandemic. However, national figures alone cannot tell us whether these improvements have been experienced equally across different population groups.

2. Gender Employment Gap

Hover over the points to see the exact gender gap for each year.

The difference between male and female employment rates has become smaller, although it has not disappeared. This suggests progress towards more balanced employment rates, but gender is only one aspect to consider. Employment also varies considerably depending on age.

3. Employment by Age

Hover over an age group to compare its employment rates and see the change from 2019 to 2025.

Employment rates vary considerably across age groups, and the amount of change since 2019 is not the same for everyone. Age is particularly interesting because employment patterns can change throughout people's working lives. For example, many people aged 20–24 may still be studying or transitioning into the workforce, which is worth keeping in mind when comparing their employment rates with those of older age groups.

Beyond age and gender, education also introduces another important difference.

4. Employment by Education

Employment Rates Remain Far Apart Across Education Levels

Employment rates by highest educational attainment in Ireland, 2019–2025

The differences are particularly pronounced across education levels. People with higher qualifications consistently have higher employment rates, and educational attainment can influence the types of jobs available to someone. Although the employment gap has fluctuated since 2019, it remains substantial, suggesting that improvements in Ireland's labour market have not benefited all education groups equally.

So far, we've explored how employment differs between population groups, but what happens when we look at where people live instead?

5. Regional Employment Differences

Regional differences tell a somewhat different story. Where people live can influence the employment opportunities available to them, but the rates across Ireland's regions have become more similar over time. This shows that not all inequalities have followed the same pattern.

What does this tell us?

Ireland's labour market has improved since 2019, and some of the gaps have become smaller. However, not everyone has experienced the same level of progress, and substantial differences remain, particularly across education levels. Overall, the figures show that a stronger labour market does not necessarily mean more equal employment outcomes.

These comparisons show how employment outcomes differ between groups, but they do not necessarily explain the reasons behind those differences.
